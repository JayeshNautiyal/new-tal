DevOps and SRE Hiring in India: What Makes It Hard

Senior DevOps and SRE roles are consistently among the hardest searches in India's engineering talent market. The sourcing challenge is real, and the assessment problem is even harder. Here is what changes when you approach these searches correctly.

Companies with live DevOps and SRE searches in India frequently discover that the talent pool looks larger than it is. A broad search for candidates with Kubernetes, Terraform, and AWS experience produces a high volume of CVs. A rigorous assessment of those CVs reveals that the depth of experience varies dramatically and that the candidates who have actually owned production infrastructure at meaningful scale are a much smaller subset of the apparent pool.

Understanding why these searches are structurally hard is the first step to approaching them in a way that produces a usable shortlist within a realistic timeline.

The certification versus experience problem

The DevOps and cloud engineering space has a well-developed certification ecosystem. AWS, Azure, GCP, Kubernetes, and HashiCorp all offer certifications that engineers acquire through structured study and examination. Many engineers in India hold multiple certifications. The certifications are real signals of tool knowledge, but they are not good proxies for the experience of running production infrastructure at scale.

A senior SRE who has been on-call for a system handling significant traffic, who has diagnosed and resolved outages at 3am, and who has driven the adoption of SLO-based reliability practices across an engineering team has experience that a Certified Kubernetes Administrator credential does not capture. The inverse is also true: many engineers who hold advanced certifications have limited production exposure at the scale and criticality that senior SRE and DevOps roles require.

CV screening for DevOps and SRE roles that uses certification density as a signal will consistently over-represent the first group and under-represent the second. The assessment methodology needs to go beyond credential recognition to probe actual production experience.

How to assess production infrastructure experience

The structured technical interview for senior DevOps and SRE candidates should centre on specific incidents and systems the candidate has owned. Useful questions include: describe the largest production system you have owned in terms of scale and complexity, what was the most significant outage you were responsible for recovering from and what caused it, how did you implement or improve on-call practices at your last company, and what infrastructure changes did you make that had the highest impact on system reliability or cost?

Strong candidates answer these questions with detail: specific traffic numbers, specific failure modes, specific changes they proposed and implemented, and honest accounts of what went wrong during migrations or scaling events. Candidates with shallow production experience answer in generalities about best practices and describe tools they have used rather than problems they have solved.

The infrastructure-as-code dimension deserves specific attention. The difference between an engineer who has maintained existing Terraform configurations and one who has designed the module structure and provider strategy for a greenfield IaC implementation is significant. Asking a candidate to walk through the architecture of the most complex infrastructure-as-code system they have built produces a quick and reliable signal about where on that spectrum they sit.

The sourcing challenge for senior SRE and platform engineers

Senior SRE and platform engineering talent in India is concentrated at a relatively small number of companies: the Indian technology firms that run large-scale consumer products, the GCCs of major global technology companies, and funded growth-stage startups with significant infrastructure requirements. Engineers at these companies are typically well-compensated, not actively looking, and selective about moving.

Sourcing them requires identifying them through their publicly visible professional output: conference talks about infrastructure problems they have solved, technical blog posts about systems they have built, open-source contributions to infrastructure tooling, and their presence in India's Site Reliability Engineering and DevOps community. The outreach built on this research is specific and credible in a way that a generic "exciting infrastructure opportunity" message is not.

The time-to-shortlist for a senior SRE or platform engineering search using passive sourcing is typically four to six weeks. Compressing that timeline by broadening the criteria or accepting candidates whose experience is shallower than the brief requires produces a short-term result and a longer-term problem.

The title confusion problem

The DevOps and SRE space has significant title inflation and role definition inconsistency across the India market. "Senior DevOps Engineer" at one company means something quite different at another. The role scope, the team structure, the production ownership, and the seniority expectations attached to a given title vary enormously between a startup, a GCC, a product company, and an IT services firm.

This matters for sourcing because a candidate's title is an unreliable indicator of their actual experience level and scope of ownership. The search brief needs to define the experience required in terms of what the candidate will have done, not what title they will have held. And the initial screening conversation needs to establish the actual scope of the candidate's current role before their profile is included in a shortlist.

What a realistic search timeline looks like

A well-run search for a senior DevOps lead or principal SRE in India runs as follows: brief development in week one, research and initial outreach in weeks two through four, first shortlist in weeks four through six, technical screening conversations in weeks five through eight, and offer and acceptance by weeks eight through eleven. Notice periods add two to three months for most senior candidates at this level.

Companies that plan infrastructure team buildouts without accounting for this lead time find themselves repeatedly behind schedule. Starting the search before the infrastructure work requires the hire, rather than at the point where the absence becomes critical, is the most reliable way to avoid that situation.
