Setting Up a GCC in India: What the Talent Search Actually Looks Like

Most GCC formation projects treat the legal entity as the primary challenge. It is not. The primary challenge is staffing the right roles in the right order, starting with a leader who can build a high-functioning team from scratch in a new market.

Global Capability Centre formation in India has accelerated significantly over the past five years. Companies that previously relied on offshore vendors or outsourcing partners are establishing owned operations, building their own engineering and product teams in Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, and Chennai, and positioning India as a core delivery location rather than a cost arbitrage play.

The legal and operational setup is well-documented and well-supported by specialist advisors. What gets underestimated — consistently — is the talent search component. Getting the entity registered is a finite, predictable task. Building a senior engineering team in a competitive talent market, starting with a leader who can attract and retain the right people, is neither finite nor predictable without the right approach.

Why the first hire determines everything

The first engineering leader you place in the GCC sets the trajectory of the entire team. They will define the technical culture, make the first architecture decisions, build or inherit the hiring process, and be the face of the organisation to every candidate who interviews in the first 18 months. A poor first hire at the engineering leadership level creates problems that compound: the wrong people get hired next, the team develops the wrong culture, and by the time the problem is visible, the cost of correction is very high.

This is why the first hire in a GCC talent search should almost never be a bulk technical hire. It should be an engineering leader, typically at Head of Engineering, VP of Engineering, or Director of Engineering level, who has the experience to build a team correctly and the credibility to attract strong individual contributors to an early-stage India presence.

The global capability centre India talent acquisition process starts with a clear brief for this leader role, not a list of technical headcount targets. The brief should define what this person needs to inherit (existing team, codebase, or a blank page), what they need to build (team structure, process, culture), and what success looks like in 12 months.

The sourcing challenge for GCC leadership roles

The candidates who are the best fit for a GCC engineering leadership role are almost universally not looking. They are currently employed at established product companies, other GCCs, or funded startups in India. They are not submitting applications to a job posting, and they will not respond meaningfully to a generic LinkedIn outreach asking them to consider "a leadership opportunity."

Reaching them requires a different approach. Effective sourcing for GCC leadership mandates involves identifying candidates through their professional network presence, their documented track record of team building, and their publicly visible contributions to engineering culture — conference talks, published engineering blogs, or known work at reference companies. The outreach is then built around what specifically makes this GCC opportunity worth their attention: the engineering problem, the mandate scope, the team they would be building, and the strategic backing of the parent company.

The sourcing window is longer for these roles than for individual contributor searches. Three to four weeks to a first credible shortlist is a realistic target if the brief is well-defined. Attempting to compress this timeline by broadening the criteria or accepting profiles that are "close enough" typically produces a first engineering leader who is not strong enough to build the team the GCC needs.

Defining the ideal candidate profile for a GCC context

GCC engineering leadership roles have specific requirements that differ from equivalent roles at a pure startup or a local product company. The successful candidate needs to be effective in a matrixed organisation where some reporting lines and decisions sit with a global headquarters, to build a team under the scrutiny of stakeholders who may have limited India market knowledge, and to attract talent to a new employer brand in a competitive city where established companies have deep recognition.

The ideal profile typically includes experience building teams from 0 to 30-plus engineers in India, comfort with ambiguity in organisational structure during a formation period, strong hiring instincts at the individual contributor and team lead level, and the communication skills to represent the India team effectively to global leadership. It does not require prior GCC experience, and candidates with that background should be evaluated on whether their previous GCC was well-run, not just whether they have the credential.

Common mistakes in GCC talent searches

The most common mistake is starting the talent search too late. GCC formation timelines are typically built around entity setup milestones. The talent search is treated as something that starts once the entity is ready. In practice, a senior engineering leadership search takes six to ten weeks from brief to day one, and bulk technical hiring behind that leader takes another three to six months to execute well. Starting the search at entity completion means the GCC is productive six to nine months later than the board expected.

The second common mistake is using a domestic recruitment agency that does not specialise in GCC formation or senior engineering search. A generalist agency working a database of active candidates will not reach the passive senior engineering leaders who are the right first hire, and will not make a credible case for the role when they do reach them. The approach to global capability centre India talent acquisition needs to match the calibre of the hire you are making.

The third mistake is building the hiring brief around a global job description rather than an India-specific brief. What a VP of Engineering does in San Francisco, Berlin, or Sydney is not identical to what the first engineering leader of a new GCC needs to do in Bangalore. The brief should reflect the actual context: the team size at start date, the decision authority the India leader has, the technical infrastructure they are inheriting, and what the global engineering team expects from the India centre in year one.

A realistic timeline for GCC talent search

For a well-run GCC talent search, plan for these phases: brief development and search initiation in weeks one and two, research and outreach in weeks two through six, first shortlist in weeks four through six, assessment and interviews in weeks six through nine, offer and acceptance in weeks nine through twelve, and notice period and start date in weeks twelve through twenty. The full cycle from brief to day one is typically four to five months for an engineering leadership hire.

The companies that build strong GCCs in India are the ones that treat the talent search as a primary workstream from the day the GCC decision is made, not an afterthought that starts once everything else is in place. The engineering team is the GCC. Everything else is infrastructure for it.
