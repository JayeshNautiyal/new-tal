IT Recruitment Agency vs Embedded RPO: Which Is Right for Your Engineering Hiring?

Both models can fill roles. The difference is in how they fill them, what candidates they access, and what happens to your engineering team's hiring quality over time. For senior engineering roles in India, the choice matters.

Talhive October 2024

The distinction between an IT recruitment agency and an embedded RPO provider is not a matter of branding. They operate with different incentive structures, different sourcing methodologies, different candidate access, and different accountability models. For companies making high-stakes senior engineering hires in India, choosing the wrong model is expensive.

This is not an argument that one model is always better than the other. Both have legitimate use cases. The argument is that the use cases are different, and conflating them produces predictable frustrations.

The core structural difference

An IT recruitment agency is a vendor. You define the role, they define the method. You pay if and when they make a placement. The agency decides which candidates to approach, how to screen them, and when to submit. You see the output at the end of the process, not during it.

An embedded RPO provider is a partner operating inside your process. They use your tools, attend your meetings, and are accountable to your hiring standards and timeline. The method is agreed jointly. The pipeline is visible throughout. You are not waiting for submissions; you are reviewing an assessed shortlist built collaboratively with a recruiter who knows your team.

The structural difference shapes every downstream outcome. It determines which candidates are in the pipeline, how well they are assessed before they reach your engineering managers, and whether the recruiter is incentivised to find the right person or the fastest person.

What an IT recruitment agency does well

IT recruitment agencies are effective when the role is well-defined, the candidate pool is active, and speed is the primary requirement. A junior to mid-level backend engineer role in a major India city where there is a reasonable supply of candidates matching the profile: a good agency can fill this faster and more cost-effectively than almost any other model.

Agencies also excel at specialist or niche searches where their existing database and established candidate relationships provide a genuine advantage. A strong technical agency with deep coverage in a specific domain, such as ML infrastructure or embedded systems, may have a database of 200 engineers with relevant experience that would take an embedded recruiter months to build from scratch.

The low upfront commitment of the contingency model also works in the agency's favour for episodic hiring. If you have one role to fill this quarter and no hiring activity anticipated for the next six months, paying a placement fee on success is more financially rational than funding a monthly capacity model.

Where IT recruitment agencies struggle with engineering hiring

The problems with the agency model for senior engineering hiring in India are structural, not accidental. They arise from the incentive architecture of contingency placement.

Senior engineers in India are predominantly passive. They are employed at good companies, not monitoring job boards, and not updating their profiles. Reaching them requires sustained outreach based on a specific and compelling case for why this role is worth their attention. Under a contingency model, the time investment required to build that case is not funded. The agency gets paid if they make a placement, which means the economics reward submitting a reasonable candidate quickly over sourcing the best candidate carefully.

The multi-agency problem compounds this. When three agencies are working on the same mandate, they all source from the same LinkedIn database using similar Boolean searches. They submit the same candidates, often simultaneously. Your engineering manager now has 12 CVs in their inbox, seven of which are the same people submitted by different agencies. The coordination overhead of managing this falls entirely on your team.

For RPO in India specifically: the volume of unqualified or duplicate submissions from contingency agencies working simultaneously on senior engineering roles is one of the most consistent frustrations reported by engineering leaders.

What embedded RPO does differently

Embedded RPO changes the sourcing model, the assessment model, and the accountability model simultaneously.

On sourcing: the embedded recruiter builds a sourcing thesis before any outreach begins. This identifies where the best candidates for this specific role currently work, what signals indicate relevant depth, and what a compelling outreach message looks like. The result is access to a candidate pool that agency database searches do not reach.

On assessment: every candidate who reaches the shortlist has been assessed by the embedded recruiter through a structured brief call. The shortlist includes a written recommendation per candidate: why they are being put forward, what the concern is, what the interview priority is. Your engineering managers review recommendations, not raw CVs.

On accountability: the embedded recruiter is measured against pipeline health, shortlist quality, and hiring manager satisfaction, not placement speed. They have no incentive to submit a fast candidate over a good one.

IT Recruitment Agency

Contingency model. You pay per placement. Incentive is speed. Candidates are primarily active. Assessment is light. Coordination overhead is yours.

Embedded RPO

Capacity model. You pay for process. Incentive is quality. Candidates include passive. Assessment is structured. Coordination is absorbed by the recruiter.

Cost comparison: the honest picture

IT agency placement fees for permanent senior engineering roles in India typically run at 8-15% of first-year CTC. For a senior engineer at 40 lakhs per annum total cash, that is 3.2 to 6 lakhs per placement. Five such hires in a year produces a placement fee total of 16 to 30 lakhs, before accounting for the cost of engineering manager time spent managing the process.

Embedded RPO runs on a monthly capacity or milestone-based fee model, spread across the engagement. For a company making five to eight senior engineering hires per year, the embedded RPO cost-per-hire typically comes in lower than the equivalent agency placement fees, after the coordination overhead and engineering manager time cost are included in the comparison.

But cost is secondary to quality. A bad senior hire costs more than either model's fee. The economic case for embedded RPO is strongest when you factor in the full cost of the wrong hire: the months of reduced productivity, the exit process, the re-search, and the impact on team morale. When to choose an IT recruitment agency and when to choose embedded RPO

Choose an IT recruitment agency when: you have a single role to fill, it is at junior to mid-level, the active candidate pool is reasonable, you need speed over depth, and the placement fee is an acceptable cost relative to the search volume.

Choose embedded RPO when: you have three or more senior engineering or leadership roles open simultaneously, the candidate pool is predominantly passive, you are running hiring across a sustained period of one quarter or more, and quality of hire is the primary metric. The Embedded RPO model is the right choice for most technology companies doing sustained senior engineering hiring in India.

For leadership and C-suite roles, a dedicated search model with milestone-based fees is typically more appropriate than either contingency agency or embedded RPO, given the complexity, timeline, and stakes of those searches.

Some companies use both in parallel: embedded RPO for senior IC and leadership roles requiring passive sourcing, and a specialist agency for mid-level roles where active candidates are available. The key is clarity about which model handles which type of role, and avoiding the situation where both are sourcing the same candidates simultaneously.
