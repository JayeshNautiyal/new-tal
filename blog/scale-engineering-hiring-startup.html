How to Scale Engineering Hiring from 5 to 50 Without an Internal TA Team

The Startup Scaling Trap

You have five engineers and a product that is starting to work. The roadmap has doubled in scope, three engineers are maxed out, and your best candidate from last month accepted an offer elsewhere because the process took six weeks. So you do what feels logical: you decide it is time to hire a recruiter.

Then you spend three months trying to find a good one. The recruiter you eventually hire spends their first two months learning the domain, building a process from scratch, and figuring out what a senior backend engineer at your company actually looks like. Meanwhile, the engineering backlog grows and your CTO is still doing interviews on Saturdays.

This is the startup scaling trap. The solution to a hiring velocity problem is not always adding headcount to the hiring function. For companies between roughly 5 and 50 engineers, there is often a faster, more capital-efficient model available: embedded Recruitment Process Outsourcing.

Why Internal TA Is Not Always the Right First Move

Building an internal talent acquisition function makes clear sense once you are hiring at sustained volume, across multiple specialisations, consistently, throughout the year. Before that point, the economics rarely work in your favour.

Consider what it actually takes to get an internal recruiter to full productivity. You need to hire them, onboard them into your technical context, give them the tools and access they need, align them with your hiring managers, and establish a process they can run. In a domain like engineering, where role complexity is high and candidate quality signals are non-obvious, this ramp can take three to five months. During that window, your hiring velocity has not improved.

There is also a sequencing problem. Early-stage startups often do not yet have the process infrastructure that makes an internal recruiter effective. No structured interview process, no ATS properly configured, no sourcing playbook, no job description template that converts. Hiring a recruiter before building that infrastructure means you are paying recruiter salaries to figure out the basics.

"We hired a recruiter at series A. They were productive by the time we closed series B."

A CTO's honest reflection on the onboarding gap

This is not an argument against internal TA. It is an argument that timing matters and that there is a more appropriate tool for each stage of growth.

The Embedded RPO Model for Scaling Startups

Embedded Recruitment Process Outsourcing services work differently from agency contingency recruiting. Rather than placing an external recruiter who works on your roles from a distance, the embedded model integrates a dedicated recruiter directly into your team. They attend your standups, use your ATS, run your process, and build your pipeline, typically within a structured engagement period rather than an open-ended, ongoing cost.

For a startup scaling from 5 to 50 engineers, this matters for several reasons. First, it compresses the time between deciding to hire and having a functioning hiring engine. A well-structured embedded RPO engagement can have an experienced recruiter running active sourcing within 10 to 14 days of kick-off. Second, it brings a sourcing infrastructure that a single internal recruiter would take months to build: vetted Boolean strings, tested outreach cadences, an existing warm network of engineers, and access to platforms that are expensive to maintain individually. Third, it is cost-bounded. You know what a three-month or six-month engagement will cost. An internal hire is open-ended.

Structuring for Scale: Three Phases

The trajectory from 5 engineers to 50 does not happen uniformly. It typically moves through three distinct phases, each with different hiring demands and different optimal structures.

1
Phase 1  ·  Engineers 5 to 15
Proof-of-concept hiring

You are hiring slowly and carefully. Each engineer will still be interviewed by the founder or CTO. Process is light, evaluation is relationship-driven, and volume is low. An embedded recruiter on a short engagement is ideal here: focused sourcing on a handful of critical roles, fast shortlists, no infrastructure overhead.

2
Phase 2  ·  Engineers 15 to 35
Process-building phase

Hiring volume is increasing and the ad-hoc approach is breaking down. Interview coordination is consuming engineering time. Offer acceptance rates are inconsistent. This phase demands both sourcing capacity and process infrastructure. Embedded RPO services with a process component are well-suited here: standardised briefs, structured interview guides, ATS configuration, and active pipeline management across multiple concurrent roles. This is also when building an India engineering team often becomes relevant for cost and talent pool reasons.

3
Phase 3  ·  Engineers 35 to 50
Transition and handover

By this stage, you have the volume and consistency to justify an internal TA hire. But the embedded model can continue to support the transition: the recruiter has built your playbook, calibrated your criteria, and trained your hiring managers. Handing over to an internal team now is clean rather than starting from scratch.

What You Need Ready Before You Start

Embedded RPO compresses time-to-hire significantly, but it is not a black box. The quality of your output is directly connected to the clarity of your input. Before engaging an embedded model, the following should be in place or built in the first week of onboarding.

  • A hiring brief per open role that covers not just skills but the problem the hire is solving, the team they are joining, and the signals that indicate strong fit. Vague JDs produce vague shortlists.
  • A designated hiring manager per role who is available for calibration in the first week. One conversation before sourcing starts saves two weeks of misdirected outreach.
  • ATS access or willingness to set one up. An embedded recruiter needs a system to log candidates, track stages, and make the pipeline visible to your team.
  • Clear interview stages, even if minimal. A two-stage process with a defined technical screen and a culture conversation is enough to begin.
  • Offer bandwidth. You need to know who can approve and extend an offer, and within what timeframe. The most common source of candidate loss is a shortlist that moves fast and an offer process that does not.

When to Transition to Internal TA

Embedded RPO is not a permanent state. It is a tool appropriate for a particular growth window. Knowing when to transition to internal TA is part of using it well.

The signal is usually consistent volume rather than a specific headcount. If you are reliably hiring five or more engineers per quarter, across more than two or three role types, with no major seasonal gaps, the economics of internal TA begin to improve. The infrastructure your embedded recruiter has built, the briefs, the sourcing playbooks, the interview guides, become the foundation for your internal team rather than a replacement for one.
