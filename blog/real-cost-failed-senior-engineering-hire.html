The Real Cost of a Failed Senior Engineering Hire

The fee for replacing a failed senior hire is only part of the cost. The full accounting includes team morale, project delay, damaged employer brand, and the compounding difficulty of the next search. Most companies underestimate the total significantly.

The conversation about hiring investment often focuses on the search fee: what does it cost to engage a search partner for a VP Engineering or senior AI role? That is a reasonable question, but it is not the most important cost variable in the decision. The more important cost is the one that arrives when the hire does not work out within twelve months.

Failed senior engineering hires are common enough that most engineering organisations have experienced one. The event is painful, it is expensive, and the full cost is rarely calculated carefully because doing so is uncomfortable. A realistic accounting of that cost changes how companies should think about investment in getting the hire right the first time.

The direct costs of a failed senior hire

The direct costs are the easiest to count. A replacement search fee for a VP Engineering or Head of Engineering role in India runs approximately the same as the original search fee, since you are running the same process again. If the first search was done through a contingency agency, you may have paid that fee on a hire who left or was exited within a year. The replacement search often goes to a different, more specialist partner, which means you pay again.

Severance for a senior engineering leader who exits within twelve to eighteen months is a real cost. Depending on how long they were employed and the terms of their agreement, this can be significant. Legal costs, if the exit is contested, add to this.

The onboarding investment in the failed hire is also a direct cost that is rarely counted. Senior hires take three to six months to reach full productivity. The management time, the technical orientation, the introduction to stakeholders, and the ramp-up support for a hire who leaves or is exited before they have contributed their full value represents a real financial loss, even if it does not appear as a line item.

The indirect costs: team disruption and project delay

The indirect costs of a failed senior engineering hire are harder to quantify but typically larger than the direct costs. A senior engineering leader who is not performing to expectations affects the team under them in specific ways. Hiring decisions they made during their tenure need to be reviewed. Technical decisions they influenced may need to be revisited. Team members who were aligned with the departing leader's approach may disengage or leave. The team that the next engineering leader inherits is often less stable than the team the first leader inherited.

Project delay is the most significant indirect cost for many companies. A senior engineering leader who joins with a mandate to ship a major product update, build out a new team, or implement a technology transition and who fails to deliver that mandate adds the delay cost to the direct hire cost. For product companies and GCCs where India centre delivery is on the critical path for global product roadmaps, that delay has quantifiable commercial consequences.

The second-order effects on subsequent hiring

A less obvious but very real cost of a failed senior engineering hire is the effect on the subsequent search. The employer brand in the India engineering talent market is partly built through candidate and employee experience. A senior engineering leader who leaves within a year, or who is exited, generates a signal in the market. Candidates who were in the pipeline for that hire and declined hear about the outcome through their networks. The next engineering leadership search starts from a slightly weaker position than the first one did.

This effect compounds when a company has had two or three failed senior hires in rapid succession. The organisation becomes known in engineering circles as a place where senior leaders do not stay, which is a self-reinforcing reputation problem. The best passive candidates, who have other options, apply that knowledge when evaluating whether to have a conversation at all.

What the total cost calculation implies for hiring investment

A conservative estimate of the total cost of a failed senior engineering hire, accounting for direct costs and a subset of the indirect costs, typically lands at three to five times the annual compensation for the role. For a VP Engineering role with a 50 lakh per year package, that is 1.5 to 2.5 crore in total cost for a hire that does not work out.

That context changes the calculation about investment in getting the hire right. A specialist search partner who charges a higher fee but delivers a materially stronger shortlist, runs a deeper assessment process, and has a better track record of placements that work out is not a premium option. They are a cost reduction strategy relative to a cheaper process that has a higher failure rate.

The decision to use a generalist agency or a database-heavy process to save on the search fee makes sense if the quality of the resulting hire is equivalent. The evidence consistently suggests it is not, particularly for senior technical roles where the best candidates are passive, the assessment is complex, and the brief requires specialist knowledge to execute. The economics favour investing in the process, every time.
