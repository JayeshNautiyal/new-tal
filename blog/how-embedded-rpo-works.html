How Embedded RPO Works: From Brief to Shortlist in 14 Days

Most companies understand what embedded RPO is, but not what it actually does in the first two weeks. This is the process from the moment an engagement starts through the first assessed shortlist, explained step by step.

Talhive Oct 28, 2026

The promise of Embedded RPO is a faster, higher-quality hiring process. But for most engineering leaders who have not worked with an embedded model before, the mechanics are unclear. What does the embedded recruiter actually do each day? What does the company need to provide? What should a shortlist look like, and when does it arrive?

This guide walks through the first 14 days of a Talhive embedded RPO engagement, stage by stage, so you know exactly what to expect before you start.

Why the first 14 days matter most

The quality of week one determines the quality of month three. This is not a figure of speech. Every structural problem that will affect a search pipeline originates in the brief development phase. A vague brief produces vague sourcing, which produces a vague shortlist, which produces a frustrated hiring manager who says the recruiter is not delivering.

The reality is that the recruiter can only work with what the brief gives them. When that brief is a job description copy-pasted from a previous search, the sourcing strategy will be generic. When it is a proper brief that captures the specific technical problem, the team context, and the compelling reason a strong candidate should care, the sourcing strategy is precise.

The two-week window is also a diagnostic window. By day 14, you will know whether the engagement is calibrated correctly. The first shortlist tells you whether the recruiter understood the brief. The hiring manager's reaction to it tells the recruiter whether the brief was accurate. That calibration loop, run early, prevents the slow drift that afflicts searches that do not build feedback systematically.

The 14-day process, stage by stage

  • 1

    Days 1 to 3: Immersion

    The embedded recruiter joins the company's Slack or Teams workspace, gets access to the ATS and any shared hiring documents, and schedules one-on-one sessions with each hiring manager. These are not briefing calls in the traditional sense. They are conversations designed to understand the team's technical context, the dynamics between the people already there, the problems the incoming hire will own, and the environment they will be stepping into. The output is not a job description. It is a set of notes that will form the foundation of a sourcing thesis.

  • 2

    Days 4 to 7: Sourcing Thesis Development

    The sourcing thesis is the document that separates an embedded RPO search from a generic agency mandate. It defines, in writing, three things: where the best candidates for this role are likely to be found (specific companies, communities, and signals, not just "LinkedIn"), what will attract them to this opportunity (the compelling case, grounded in what is actually true about this company, not marketing language), and what will differentiate the outreach in a market where every engineering leader receives multiple messages per week. Most agencies skip this step entirely and move straight to Boolean searches. The sourcing thesis takes longer upfront and produces materially better pipelines downstream.

  • 3

    Days 7 to 10: Active Sourcing

    With the sourcing thesis written and reviewed by the hiring manager, active sourcing begins. This involves LinkedIn and GitHub sourcing based on the specific targets identified in the thesis, outreach to relevant engineering communities and networks, and personalised first-contact messages that reference the candidate's specific work or background. The target by day 10 is 60 to 80 relevant candidates identified and 20 to 30 in active conversation. These numbers are the output of thesis-based sourcing, not spray-and-pray outreach.

  • 4

    Days 10 to 14: Shortlist Assembly and Calibration

    Screening calls run with candidates who have expressed interest and passed an initial assessment. The embedded recruiter is evaluating technical depth, motivation, and fit against the sourcing thesis, not just availability and rough profile match. The first three to four candidates who clear screening are shared with the hiring manager as a calibration shortlist: not the final list, but enough to get directional feedback on whether the search is on track. Each candidate comes with a written assessment covering strengths, concerns, and the recruiter's recommendation. RPO in India engagements that skip this calibration step typically need to rebuild the pipeline from scratch after six weeks of misaligned submissions.

What a proper shortlist looks like

A shortlist from an embedded RPO engagement is not a stack of CVs. Each candidate entry includes a written assessment structured around the brief: what they have built that is relevant, what their motivations are for considering a move, where the genuine question marks are, and a clear recommendation from the embedded recruiter on whether to advance to a first interview.

The hiring manager's job at shortlist stage is to evaluate those recommendations and provide calibration feedback, not to re-screen from scratch. If a hiring manager is spending more than 30 minutes per candidate at shortlist review, the shortlist has not been assessed enough before delivery.

The distinction between an assessed shortlist and a CV dump matters enormously in practice. An assessed shortlist with five candidates requires two to three hours of hiring manager time to review. A CV dump with fifteen profiles requires eight to ten. The embedded model pays for itself partly in the time it returns to engineering leaders who should be building product, not screening candidates.

What happens after the first shortlist

Once calibration feedback comes back from the hiring manager, the search strategy is adjusted if needed and the pipeline continues to build. Interview scheduling and coordination move to the embedded recruiter: they manage the candidate's experience through each stage, maintain regular touchpoints so candidates do not go dark, and surface problems early when a candidate's enthusiasm is cooling or a competing offer is materialising.

Offer management is one of the stages where embedded RPO consistently outperforms agency models. The embedded recruiter has full context on both sides: they know what the company can offer and what it cannot, and they have spent weeks in conversation with the candidate, understanding what matters to them. That context makes the close significantly more reliable than an agency making a call after four weeks of arm's-length involvement.

Pipeline reporting runs weekly throughout the engagement. Hiring managers and leadership receive a regular snapshot of funnel health: how many candidates are at each stage, what the conversion rates are, where the bottlenecks are, and what actions are being taken to address them. This visibility is one of the clearest structural advantages of Recruitment Process Outsourcing services over a multi-agency model, where pipeline data is fragmented across multiple vendor relationships and nobody owns the whole picture.

The variables that accelerate or slow the process

The 14-day shortlist timeline assumes certain inputs are in place. Hiring managers who are available for the immersion sessions in week one, can provide feedback on the sourcing thesis within 24 to 48 hours, and review the calibration shortlist within two to three days of delivery will consistently see shortlists delivered on or ahead of schedule.

The most common source of delay is not sourcing difficulty. It is feedback latency. When a hiring manager takes ten days to review a four-candidate shortlist, the pipeline stalls. Candidates who were warm at the point of submission may have accepted other offers by the time the calibration call happens. An embedded RPO engagement requires a modest but consistent time investment from the hiring side: one hour per week per active search, and rapid feedback on shortlist submissions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most embedded RPO engagements can begin within one to two weeks of agreement. The first week is immersion and brief development. Active sourcing begins in week two. A first assessed shortlist is usually ready within 14 days of the engagement starting, assuming the hiring manager is available for the immersion sessions.
A sourcing thesis is a written document that defines where the best candidates for a specific role are likely to be, what will attract them, and what will differentiate the company in outreach. It replaces generic Boolean search strings with a considered, specific strategy. The quality of the sourcing thesis is the primary determinant of shortlist quality downstream.
A well-run embedded RPO engagement typically delivers a first shortlist of four to six assessed candidates within 14 days. These are not all candidates identified, but those who have passed an initial screening call and been assessed against the brief. Quality over volume is the goal: four well-assessed candidates are more useful than fifteen unscreened CVs.
A first shortlist that does not match expectations is a calibration signal, not a failure. It means the sourcing thesis needs refinement based on specific feedback from the hiring manager. A good embedded RPO partner treats shortlist feedback as the most important input of the entire engagement and adjusts the search strategy accordingly. The calibration loop, run early, prevents the slow drift that affects poorly structured searches.
