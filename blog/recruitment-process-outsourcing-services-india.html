Why Global Companies Are Turning to RPO Services in India

India produces roughly 1.5 million engineering graduates annually, and its product engineering ecosystem has matured significantly over the last decade. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Chennai now host engineering teams for hundreds of global product companies, from early-stage startups to publicly listed tech firms. The cost advantage that originally drove offshore hiring decisions is now secondary to the talent quality argument.

This shift has changed what global companies need from their recruiting partners. The old model, bulk hiring through staffing agencies optimised for headcount, does not work for product engineering. Companies building distributed teams that will work alongside London or New York counterparts need recruiters who understand product culture, can assess engineering judgment, and know how to position an overseas brand to the best candidates in a market where options are plentiful.

That is the gap that Recruitment Process Outsourcing services in India are increasingly being asked to fill.

What RPO in India Actually Covers

The term RPO covers a wide spectrum of engagement models. Understanding what you are buying matters before you start evaluating providers.

At one end of the spectrum, enterprise RPO involves a provider taking over the entire talent acquisition function for a large organisation: process design, technology, sourcing, screening, compliance, and reporting. This is typically a multi-year contract with a large team embedded into the client's HR infrastructure.

At the other end, embedded RPO places a single dedicated recruiter inside a smaller or faster-moving team. This recruiter runs sourcing and hiring for a defined set of roles, operates inside the client's tools and process, and functions as an extension of the founding or engineering team rather than a vendor. For most product companies hiring in India, this embedded model is more appropriate than the enterprise alternative.

Between them sits project RPO: a time-bounded engagement to fill a defined number of roles in a short window. This is often used for hiring sprints around a funding event or a new product launch.

The Indian Engineering Market: What Buyers Need to Know

Sourcing engineering talent in India has specific characteristics that differ from Western markets and that affect what makes an RPO provider effective here.

Passive candidate density is high. India's best engineers are rarely actively job hunting. LinkedIn profiles are often incomplete or outdated. Most strong hires come through warm outreach, referral networks, and targeted sourcing by recruiters who have built relationships in specific technical communities over time.

Compensation benchmarks move fast. Salaries at the senior and principal engineer level in Bengaluru and Hyderabad have moved significantly over the last three years. A provider relying on benchmarks from 2022 will either underprice offers and lose candidates or overbuild expectations with clients. Real-time market intelligence is a core capability, not a bonus.

Brand is a real variable. Candidates compare offers across multiple companies. For a global company hiring in India for the first time, the absence of brand recognition in the local market requires active work: a clear employer value proposition, evidence of team quality, and a recruiter who can represent the company authentically. Recruiters who work only on JD-and-apply models will not solve this problem.

Services vs. product background matters. India has a large services engineering sector. Candidates from services backgrounds are not automatically less strong, but they require different evaluation: the signals for strong product engineering judgment differ from those for strong delivery execution. RPO providers who have not done this distinction clearly at screening will pollute your pipeline with strong services engineers for product roles.

Eight Criteria for Evaluating RPO Services in India

Use this framework when assessing any provider for engineering hiring in India.

1

Named recruiter commitment

Will you know who is working on your account before you sign? Embedded RPO depends on individual relationships and context. Providers who cannot commit a specific person are staffing, not embedding.

2

Depth in engineering, not just tech

Recruiting for software engineers is not the same as recruiting for IT support. Does the provider have a track record placing backend engineers, ML practitioners, or DevOps engineers specifically? Ask for examples.

3

Product company experience

Providers with a large services-company client base will have sourcing networks calibrated to that market. For product engineering roles, you want someone who has placed engineers at funded startups and product-led companies, not IT outsourcers.

4

Live compensation data

Ask what backend engineer salaries look like at senior level in Bengaluru right now. If they hesitate or quote a range wider than 30%, their market intelligence is stale.

5

Sourcing methodology

Do they source proactively or rely on job board applications? Describe their sourcing thesis concept. A provider that responds with a Boolean string rather than a sourcing strategy is working at the wrong level.

6

Time-to-shortlist track record

What is their average time from brief to first shortlist for engineering roles? Anything beyond three weeks for a senior IC role suggests process or network gaps.

7

Cross-timezone working model

If your hiring managers are in Europe or the US, how does the recruiter handle coordination across time zones? A recruiter who only works IST with no async handoff capability will create friction at every stage.

8

Commercial structure clarity

Are the fees milestone-based or monthly? Is there a minimum commitment period? Are there terms around re-hiring candidates they placed within a certain window? All of this should be clear before you proceed.

What Good RPO Looks Like vs. What You Usually Get

Strong provider

  • First shortlist within 10 to 14 days, pre-screened
  • Weekly pipeline review with pipeline health metrics
  • Candidate communication written to your brand voice
  • Calibration after first shortlist tightens the second

Common gaps

  • Sourcing is job-board dependent, no warm pipeline
  • First shortlist arrives after four weeks with generic CVs
  • No pipeline visibility between weekly calls
  • Candidate outreach is templated, not contextualised
  • Same shortlist quality problems repeat after feedback
  • No active tracking of offer-stage competitor activity

Structuring the Engagement for Success

The most common reason RPO engagements underperform is not provider quality. It is how the engagement is structured at the outset. Global companies hiring in India for the first time often underinvest in the onboarding stage, assuming the provider will figure things out on their own.

The first week of an engagement should include a brief per open role that goes beyond skills to include team context, hiring manager style, what has not worked in previous hiring for this role, and a clear statement of what success looks like. It should include time with the hiring manager, not just an HR contact. And it should result in a written sourcing thesis that the client has reviewed before sourcing begins.

If an embedded RPO provider is not asking for this level of onboarding, that itself is a signal.
