Why India Is Different as a Hiring Market

International hiring leaders often arrive in India with assumptions built on their experience in other markets, and those assumptions fail in predictable ways.

The first failure is underestimating candidate leverage at the senior engineering level. Bengaluru is one of the most competitive engineering talent markets in the world at the four to ten years of experience level. Senior engineers in product-oriented roles receive significant outreach volume from both domestic and international companies. They have options, and they know it.

The second failure is applying compensation frameworks from other markets without India market calibration. Companies that benchmark against their London or US engineering salaries and convert at a fixed ratio consistently find themselves below the relevant competitive range for experienced product engineers in Bangalore or Hyderabad. India compensation is not simply a discounted version of Western rates; it has its own structure, its own equity norms, and its own growth trajectory over the last several years.

The third failure is treating India as a homogeneous market. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, and the Delhi NCR cluster have different talent profiles, different compensation norms, different candidate preferences about company type, and meaningfully different engineering cultures. An RPO provider with genuine depth in India will help you calibrate by city as well as by discipline.

The Landscape of RPO Providers in India

The market for Recruitment Process Outsourcing services in India has grown considerably over the last decade, and the range of provider types reflects that growth. Understanding the landscape helps you identify where your needs are most likely to be met well.

Large-Scale RPO Firms
Volume-oriented, process-heavy

Global RPO players with large India operations. Strong for high-volume hiring across standardised roles. Less suited to nuanced engineering searches or early-stage companies that need a recruiter who can operate with significant autonomy.

Staffing Agencies
Transactional, placement-focused

Traditional staffing firms covering technology roles. They operate on a per-placement fee model and do not embed within client teams. Higher cost per hire and lower continuity than a structured RPO engagement.

Technology-Specialist RPO
Domain-deep, embedded model

Smaller firms focused exclusively on technology hiring. Recruiters have deep domain knowledge in engineering disciplines. Work embedded within client teams with continuity across the engagement. Better suited to product companies and global technology teams.

For global technology companies building product engineering teams in India, the technology-specialist embedded RPO model delivers significantly better outcomes than either volume RPO or transactional staffing. The reasons are structural: domain expertise matters when screening engineering candidates, and continuity matters when building employer brand in a market where candidate networks are tight and word travels.

Domain Expertise Is the Critical Variable

The India technology hiring market rewards domain expertise more than most. Engineering candidates at the senior level are sophisticated evaluators of whether a recruiter understands the role they are discussing. A recruiter who cannot articulate the difference between a platform engineering role and a backend application engineering role, or who cannot engage credibly with a candidate about their technical work, damages the employer brand of the company they represent.

When evaluating RPO providers for India technology hiring, the most important question to ask is not about their process or their database. It is about the experience level of the individual recruiters who will actually be conducting outreach and running screens. Ask specifically:

  • How many years have the individual recruiters worked exclusively in technology hiring?
  • What engineering disciplines do they have the deepest sourcing experience in?
  • Can you speak with an engineer who was placed by a specific recruiter on their team?
  • How do they approach sourcing for a role where there is no obvious candidate pool on LinkedIn?

Providers who cannot answer these questions with specificity are running a generalist operation dressed in technology language. The distinction matters enormously for outcomes.

Understanding the India Engineering Candidate Pool

The India engineering talent pool has particular characteristics that shape how effective RPO providers approach it.

The IIT/NIT premium is real but overstated. Candidates from the top engineering institutions carry strong brand recognition among Indian hiring teams, and that recognition can be a useful proxy for early-career talent. At the five-plus years of experience level, however, product and architecture quality matters significantly more than educational pedigree. Effective recruiters look for demonstrated output rather than institutional affiliation.

The startup alumni pool is large and undervalued. India has produced a substantial class of engineers who have worked at high-growth product companies across consumer internet, fintech, B2B SaaS, and deep tech. Many of these engineers have worked across multiple startup cycles and developed engineering breadth that a corporate career path does not produce. For global product companies, this pool is often a better source of candidates than engineers from large services organisations.

Passive candidates require a different sourcing approach. The strongest engineering candidates in India, particularly in Bengaluru's product ecosystem, are rarely actively applying. They are open to conversations, but they need to be approached with relevance and specificity rather than generic job descriptions. This is where domain expertise and a warm network translate directly into sourcing results. An RPO provider who relies primarily on job postings and inbound applications will not reach this candidate pool effectively.

How Global Companies Should Structure an India RPO Engagement

The structural choices you make at the start of an RPO engagement in India have a significant effect on outcomes. These are the decisions that matter most.

Define scope by discipline, not by headcount

Telling an RPO provider you want to hire twenty engineers in India is less useful than telling them you need eight backend engineers with distributed systems experience, six data engineers with pipeline and infrastructure background, and six product managers who have worked in B2B SaaS. The discipline-level definition shapes how the provider organises sourcing, which recruiters they deploy, and how they calibrate compensation.

Give the RPO team direct access to engineering stakeholders

The most effective India RPO engagements are those where the embedded recruiter has direct access to engineering managers, tech leads, or a CTO for brief calibration and feedback. Routing everything through an HR coordinator creates information loss and slows down the calibration that produces quality candidates. See the full detail on this in how embedded RPO works.

Invest in employer brand positioning before sourcing starts

Strong India engineering candidates evaluate companies on product quality, team composition, and engineering culture before they evaluate compensation. An RPO provider cannot compensate for an employer brand that is unknown or poorly positioned in the India market. Before a sourcing campaign begins, ensure the company's technical blog, engineering culture documentation, and interview process are set up to convert interested candidates into serious ones.

Build in a pipeline review cadence

India RPO engagements that run without a weekly pipeline review meeting tend to drift. Candidates move quickly in the India market; without a regular forum for the recruiter and hiring managers to calibrate on what is working and what is not, sourcing quality degrades over time. Structure a standing thirty-minute weekly review as part of the engagement from the start.

Common Mistakes Global Companies Make

1
Applying a single compensation range to the entire India geography

Bengaluru compensation for senior engineers is materially higher than Pune or Chennai for the same role. A single India-wide range will either overpay in secondary cities or underpay in Bengaluru, where the strongest product engineering talent is concentrated.

2
Choosing an RPO provider based on size rather than domain fit

Large RPO firms with significant India headcount often lack the engineering domain depth to source effectively for senior product roles. Size is a proxy for process capability, not for domain expertise.

3
Running a slow or opaque interview process

Candidate experience matters in the India market. Senior engineers who are evaluating multiple options will withdraw from a process that takes more than three weeks from first screen to offer, or where feedback is not given promptly. RPO providers cannot compensate for a broken interview process; they can only source as well as the process they are feeding into.

4
Not investing in the job brief before sourcing begins

A generic job description written for a global job board is not a brief for an embedded recruiter. Without a clear articulation of what makes this role interesting, what the engineering challenge is, and what the team context looks like, the recruiter cannot write compelling outreach or screen candidates against what actually matters.

5
Treating India hiring as a cost arbitrage exercise

Companies that position India as a cost reduction strategy in their employer brand, whether explicitly or through the structure of the roles they offer, consistently struggle to attract strong product-oriented engineers. The most effective India engineering teams are built by companies who position India as a primary engineering location, not a satellite cost centre.

Fee and Commercial Structure

The commercial models for RPO services in India vary across provider types, and understanding the tradeoffs helps you negotiate a structure that aligns incentives correctly. For a detailed breakdown across models, the RPO pricing models guide covers this in full.

The key principle for India RPO engagements is that models which create alignment between provider activity and client hiring outcomes outperform those that incentivise volume activity without quality accountability. Specifically:

  • Monthly embedded fee structures work well when there is a sustained pipeline of roles over a defined period and the RPO team is operating with true embedded access to engineering stakeholders. They create continuity and allow the recruiter to develop deep institutional knowledge.
  • Per-hire milestone structures work well for companies with a defined set of roles and a preference for outcome-based accountability. They require clear definition of what constitutes a successful placement to avoid disputes on milestone triggers.
  • Hybrid structures combining a base engagement fee with per-hire components are increasingly common in the India market for technology hiring, as they reflect the genuine fixed cost of embedded recruiting work alongside the outcome component.

Be cautious about purely per-placement models for senior engineering roles. The incentive for the provider is to move quickly to a placement rather than to find the best possible candidate, and that tension becomes particularly costly when the role is a leadership or founding-team position where the wrong hire has compounding effects.
